ED Boarding: Mortality Impact Calculator

Illustrative values. The relative risk and data-range constants in this page are placeholders, not study results.

This calculator visualises results from the UNCORKED study on emergency department boarding and mortality. (The study is currently in press; a link will be added when it is available online.)

Boarded patients are those who have been admitted but remain in the emergency department while waiting for an inpatient bed. Boarding occupancy is the number of boarded patients divided by the number of ED treatment spaces (cubicles). The analysis assessed whether boarding occupancy when a patient arrived was associated with 28-day mortality among patients admitted from the ED, adjusting for age, sex, diagnosis group, hospital type and recruitment snapshot, with allowance for clustering of patients within hospitals.

The calculator shows the relative risk of 28-day mortality for admitted patients at the entered level of boarding, compared with a reference level that you set (by default, no boarded patients), with a 95% confidence interval.

Disclaimer: This is an observational association, not proof that boarding causes the difference in risk. The calculator is intended only to visualise the study results; further work is needed before it could be used as a clinical or operational tool.

Method: the change in risk is calculated from the study's relative risk per 0.1 increase in boarding occupancy (10 additional boarded patients per 100 treatment spaces), applied multiplicatively across the difference between the entered and reference levels. This relative risk is averaged over the patients in the study, so it is an approximation for any individual department.
